About me

How I got here.

I started in 2022 as an accountant at Dulsa France, an industrial SME, while I was still studying business management. I learned the job from the ground up: supplier and customer accounts, bank reconciliations, VAT returns, inventory counts, month-end accruals.

Two years later I moved into management control, and the questions changed. Not just "are the books right?", but "what are they telling us?": which product lines make money, where the cash goes, which budget line is drifting and what to do about it. I reported directly to the CFO.

Along the way I completed a Master's in Accounting, Control and Audit, the French graduate track to chartered accountancy. In October 2026 I moved to Ohio to join Speed North America.

What I've done

The work I'm proudest of so far.

ERP · Project lead

I led our Sage X3 implementation

The challenge: the company ran on scattered legacy tools, and the finance team needed one reliable system.

What I did: configured Sage X3, migrated the data from the old tools, and trained the users so the team could actually work in it.

What I learned: an ERP project is half accounting, half people. The system only works if the data is clean and the team trusts it.

Treasury

Cash and working capital

I built the cash flow forecast and tracked working capital (DSO, DPO, DIO), then worked on getting customers to pay faster. I also assessed customer credit risk and set up follow-up of payment schedules.

FP&A

Budget and variance analysis

Every month I compared actuals to budget, explained the gaps and recommended corrective actions to the CFO. I tracked OPEX and CAPEX against approved budgets.

Profitability

Margins by product line

I analyzed margins product line by product line so management could decide where to push and where to rethink pricing.

Close & reporting

The monthly close and P&L

I ran the month-end close and produced the P&L reporting for senior management. Earlier, as an accountant, I calculated the accruals and helped prepare the annual financial statements and the cash flow statement.
